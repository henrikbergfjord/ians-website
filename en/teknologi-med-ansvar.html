THE OPPORTUNITY

From thought to something that works.

You can describe a need, build a first version and test it in practice. AI can help with code, design and explanations along the way. At IANS, I use these tools to try ideas, learn from what fails and improve what works.

More people can build

Technology that once required specialist teams can now be used by many more people. That lowers the barrier from idea to experiment and from experiment to solution.

We learn in a new way

AI can explain, suggest, challenge and help us move forward. But a good answer is not automatically a true one. Knowledge, source criticism and our own judgment therefore matter more, not less.

THE OTHER SIDE

The same power can be misused.

Tools that help us create can also be used for fraud, manipulation and automated attacks. Giving an agent access to real systems makes its boundaries and our oversight especially important.

Rules and boundaries

I think we need clear boundaries where technology can harm people, and room to explore where the risks are smaller. We should revisit the rules as we learn what works.

People must stay involved

Automation can make many things easier. I also want to ask which judgments, relationships and skills we wish to preserve, and when a person should make the final decision.

We should not only ask what technology can do.
We must also decide what we want to use it for.

OUR CHILDREN

How can we guide children as technology changes?

Children are growing up with AI as part of daily life. We can explore together, set boundaries and talk about what is real, what gets shared and whom to ask when something feels wrong. The conversation should grow with their age and experience.

Is this true?
Who made it?
Where did the information come from?
What should I share?
When should I think for myself?

I do not have a finished recipe. My aim is to stay present, keep learning myself and give children more understanding and responsibility over time.
