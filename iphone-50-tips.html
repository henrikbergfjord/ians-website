Back Tap: turn the back of your iPhone into a button

Double- or triple-tap the back of iPhone to trigger an action or Shortcut.

HOW TO DO ITSettings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap. Choose Double Tap or Triple Tap, then assign an action.
◎ Most modern iPhones
WHAT IT REALLY DOES Back Tap uses sensors inside the iPhone to recognise a deliberate double or triple tap on the back of the device. It can trigger system actions such as a screenshot or accessibility feature — and it can also run one of your Shortcuts.
REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE Try assigning Screenshot to Double Tap and a useful Shortcut to Triple Tap. That gives you two extra controls without adding anything to the Home Screen.
STEP BY STEP Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap. Choose Double Tap or Triple Tap, then assign an action.
Settings→Accessibility→Touch→Back Tap
IANS TIP Triple Tap is often better for important Shortcuts because it is less likely to be triggered accidentally.

Lock or hide sensitive apps with Face ID

Add another layer of privacy when someone else is holding your unlocked iPhone.

HOW TO DO ITTouch and hold a supported app icon, then choose Require Face ID. Where supported, you can also hide the app.
◎ Supported apps / current iOS
WHAT IT REALLY DOES App locking adds another authentication check even after the iPhone itself has been unlocked. It is useful when you temporarily hand your phone to another person but do not want every app immediately accessible.
REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE Good candidates include apps containing private conversations, personal documents, photos or financial information.
STEP BY STEP Touch and hold a supported app icon, then choose Require Face ID. Where supported, you can also hide the app.
Touch and hold app→Require Face ID
IANS TIP Think of this as an extra privacy layer, not a replacement for a strong device passcode and Face ID.

Master Camera Control

On supported iPhones, Camera Control can provide fast access to camera settings such as zoom and exposure.

HOW TO DO ITOpen Camera and use Camera Control. Available controls depend on your iPhone model and settings.
◎ Selected iPhones
WHAT IT REALLY DOES Camera Control is more than a shutter button. On supported iPhones it can open Camera, take photos, record video and expose controls including Exposure, Depth, Zoom, Cameras, Styles and Tone. Apple also supports AE/AF Lock.
REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE Lightly double-press Camera Control to show available controls, slide to the control you want, lightly press to select it and slide again to adjust it.
STEP BY STEP Open Camera and use Camera Control. Available controls depend on your iPhone model and settings.
Settings→Camera→Camera Control→Customize
IANS TIP Go to Camera Control settings and customise which controls appear. Removing controls you never use makes it much faster.

HOW TO DO ITOpen Settings → Battery → Charging and review the charging optimisation or charge-limit options available on your model.
◎ Supported iPhones

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE If your iPhone normally spends hours connected to power, a lower limit can reduce the amount of time the battery remains at a very high charge level.
STEP BY STEP Open Settings → Battery → Charging and review the charging optimisation or charge-limit options available on your model.
